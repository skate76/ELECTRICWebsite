01 — Background

The Problem

Household electricity consumption varies from month to month based on factors such as household size, appliance usage, and lifestyle habits. This variability makes it difficult for consumers to accurately estimate their monthly electricity bills, especially when operating on a fixed budget.

A household must decide how to allocate a limited amount of electricity usage across multiple appliances. Due to varying electricity rates, differing appliance power consumption, and unpredictable usage patterns, users often cannot estimate how long to run each appliance without exceeding a specified budget. These difficulties are compounded when new appliances are introduced or when electricity rates change.

Currently, no system allows users to optimize electricity usage against a specified budget while maximizing utility.

In Jamaica specifically, electricity costs are among the highest in the Caribbean, charged by the Jamaica Public Service Company (JPS) at a blended residential rate of approximately J$45.46 per kWh. This creates real financial pressure particularly for lower and middle-income households, making structured electricity budgeting a necessity.

02 — Motivation

Why This System Exists

Electricity has become a vital part of any household. As a result, electricity costs form a fixed part of every monthly budget. In Jamaica, these costs are high and can cause significant financial pressure on households, especially those with limited incomes, making budgeting a necessity for comfortable living.

Manual electricity budgeting is difficult: various appliances draw different wattages, operate for varying durations, and electricity rates are largely unknown to the average consumer. Usage patterns also vary by time of day and behavioral habits, making manual estimation rough and mostly inaccurate. Manual tracking also involves inconsistent record-keeping, provides no useful feedback, and ultimately leads to overspending and inefficient electricity use.

The motivation for this project stems from the need to provide a practical, automated way to optimize household electricity consumption to fit within a budget. An automated optimization system provides:

  • Precise calculations based on real wattage and rate data
  • Hard budget enforcement — the plan is guaranteed not to exceed the limit
  • Actionable recommendations that can realistically be followed
  • Scenario testing so households can plan ahead before committing to changes
03 — Objectives

What the System Does

The primary objective is to develop a budget-constrained electricity optimization system for Jamaican households that minimizes electricity costs while maintaining necessary appliance functionality and ensuring household budgets are respected.

Budget InputAccepts a monthly electricity budget in JMD and uses it as a hard constraint on all optimization outputs.
Optimal AllocationCalculates optimal appliance usage based on power ratings (watts/kW), maximum allowable usage time, and electricity cost per kWh.
Budget EnforcementEnsures total projected consumption stays within the specified budget; warns of overconsumption if unavoidable.
What-If AnalysisSupports dynamic scenario testing: add a new appliance, increase usage time, or change the budget to see the updated plan instantly.
Clear OutputsProduces optimal usage per appliance, cost breakdown, comparative results, and pie chart visualizations.
Sound MathematicsAllocates usage optimally by priority using a mathematically proven LP formulation with globally optimal guarantees.
04 — Technical Approach

Linear Programming as a Solution

The system models electricity as a scarce resource and formulates the problem as a Linear Programming (LP) optimization. The problem maps directly onto LP because the electricity cost function is inherently linear: Cost = (W/1000) × hours × rate × days. Both the objective (maximize priority-weighted usage) and all constraints (budget cap, maximum hours, non-negativity) are linear functions of the decision variables.

LP guarantees a globally optimal solution for problems of this scale. The system uses scipy.optimize.linprog from Python's SciPy library, implementing both the Simplex Method and the HiGHS interior-point solver. After optimization, a surplus redistribution pass caps any over-allocated appliance at its ideal cost and proportionally redistributes the freed budget to remaining appliances by priority, iterating until no surplus remains.

The Hungarian Algorithm was considered but rejected. It handles one-to-one assignment problems and cannot be applied to continuous resource allocation with inequality constraints. Greedy heuristics could approximate a solution but do not guarantee optimality.

05 — Scope & Limitations

What the System Does Not Do

The system is designed specifically as a household electricity budgeting and optimization tool. It uses parameters such as watt rating, maximum usage hours, priority level, billing days, and the fixed JPS rate to compute optimal usage distribution across appliances.

The following are explicitly outside scope:

  • Real-time electricity monitoring or smart meter integration
  • Connection to JPS or any utility provider's live systems
  • Time-of-use or peak/off-peak pricing variations (fixed rate assumed)
  • Household financial planning services or electrical system diagnosis
  • Official electricity bill generation

The system assumes a fixed electricity rate, user-provided usage estimates, and a consistent billing period. It produces recommendations that households choose to follow; it does not control any physical appliance. Future enhancements could incorporate live JPS rate feeds, time-of-use pricing, smart plug integration, and machine learning-based usage prediction.
